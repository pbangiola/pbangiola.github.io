Data work

I use data to solve practical problems for real people. My work spans educational technology, school leadership, classroom assessment, and technical documentation.

Good analysis starts with the right question and ends with something someone can actually use.

Examples are described without individual student records or confidential client information. School results are aggregate and descriptive, not controlled causal estimates.

Educational technology · Operations

Getting learners access

At American Prison Data Systems, I built and improved account-provisioning and data workflows across correctional institutions. That meant managing permissions, protecting learner information, coordinating systems, and making processes repeatable for support teams.

The result: onboarding supported thousands of learners daily, with a 24-hour turnaround, and a revised process used across 19 implementations.

K–8 curriculum leadership · Assessment

Seeing whether a school is improving

At St. Viator, I used diagnostic assessment data to guide curriculum decisions, interventions, and conversations with teachers. I tracked both achievement and growth because they answer different questions.

The result: schoolwide average math achievement rose from the 52nd to 68th percentile and reading from the 61st to 70th. Median annual growth rose from 87% to 119% of the national average in math, and from 114% to 143% in reading.

Ninth-grade English · Classroom assessment

Reading the wrong answers

When I examined grammar errors, I noticed that some reflected systematic differences between students’ home dialects and the conventions expected on standardized tests. I used those patterns to teach the differences explicitly, without treating students’ language as deficient.

The result: students gained an average of four points on the pre-ACT scale over the year, alongside broader changes to grammar and writing instruction. I also built Excel tools that saved colleagues roughly three hours a week on recurring assessment analysis.

Edtech support · Knowledge management

Making information findable

I revived a stalled documentation project and expanded it from one product to five, including client-specific variations. I wrote, organized, and published 200 pages in two months for a support operation serving teachers across 15 states.

The point: information is only useful when the person who needs it can find it, understand it, and act on it.
