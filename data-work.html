Case study 01

Education technology provider serving correctional institutions

Making learner onboarding repeatable

The problem

Learners needed access to multiple educational services in institutions with different operating requirements. Account assignment, passwords, reporting, and support had to work consistently as implementations expanded.

My work

I built an account-provisioning process, maintained a data warehouse across jurisdictions, and designed data standards that included safeguards for learners’ personally identifying information. I used R, Python, Excel, and Google Drive to prototype, deploy, and evaluate programs.

Making the process usable

I designed a way for learners to find their necessary passwords in one place, developed client reporting templates, and worked with engineering on provisioning and system stability. I also trained support associates and helped develop scripts for outsourced technical support.

What changed

The onboarding system supported thousands of learners daily with a 24-hour turnaround. The revised implementation process was repeated across 19 implementations.

The decision behind the data: scaling meant making the process repeatable for other people, with clear standards, training, and support.

Measurement and scope

My historical records also report 99.9% fidelity for a human-mediated process and 99.7% effectiveness for a prototype. These are separate measures, not two estimates of the same result. Their denominators and evaluation conditions are not documented here, so they are not presented as a directly comparable performance chart. The number of accounts and the number of learners are also distinct.

Case study 02

K–8 school · Curriculum and instructional leadership

Using assessment data to guide schoolwide change

The question

Were students making enough progress, and were the school’s curriculum and support systems meeting different learning needs? A single average could not answer both questions.

My work

I used nationally normed diagnostic assessments to track achievement and growth over multiple years. I examined results across student groups and connected those findings to curriculum adoption, interventions, and access to services.

From analysis to action

I implemented a multi-tiered system of supports, helped obtain IEP and 504 documentation for students who needed it, and led curriculum changes in math and foundational literacy. Teacher coaching helped turn the analysis into classroom practice.

Data operations alongside analysis

I also administered the student information system and Google Workspace, including account provisioning, permissions, and staff support. Managing access and maintaining useful systems were part of the same job as interpreting learning results.

Case study 03

Ninth-grade English · Assessment analysis and curriculum design

Asking what a wrong answer actually means

The problem

The supplied grammar materials treated students’ differences from standardized English as a lack of language knowledge. Looking at the questions that caused difficulty suggested a more specific instructional need.

What I noticed

Some patterns involved differences between the grammatical conventions students used in their home dialect and those expected by the test. I used that interpretation to redesign instruction rather than simply assign more of the same practice.

What I changed

I taught language differences explicitly, helping students recognize the test’s expected conventions without treating their home dialect as defective. The aim was to add a useful set of conventions to students’ existing language knowledge.

Helping colleagues use data

I also developed Excel tools and trained colleagues to streamline assessment analysis, reducing the recurring time spent on data work by approximately three hours per week.

+4.0Average pre-ACT scale-score gain, on a 36-point scale
OriginalGrammar and writing curriculum informed by question-level patterns
~3 hoursWeekly assessment-analysis time saved through Excel tools and peer training

The decision behind the data: an incorrect test response can reveal a difference in language conventions. Understanding the pattern changed what I taught and how I explained it.

Measurement and scope

The score gain is an aggregate end-of-year result from my classroom work, not an estimate of the isolated effect of dialect-aware instruction. The language interpretation describes my instructional analysis; it is not a formal linguistic study or a conclusion about every student. Test items, student responses, names, and individual assessment records are not reproduced. The time saving is an approximate workflow result, not a timed experimental comparison.

Case study 04

Education software provider · Knowledge management

Turning scattered knowledge into usable documentation

The problem

A knowledge-management project had stalled. Teachers and support staff needed clear documentation for software with multiple products and customer-specific variations.

My work

I created, organized, and uploaded documentation, expanding an initial assignment covering one product to five. I documented customer-specific variations and made the process sustainable using the existing staff.

What changed

I produced 200 pages in two months and implemented a knowledge base supporting scalable technical support for teachers across 15 states.

Why this belongs in a data portfolio

Information only helps when people can find it, understand which situation it applies to, and keep it current. Organizing the knowledge around real product and customer differences made it more useful to the people supporting learners.

How I work

Start with the decision people need to make

I work backward from the question: what do we need to understand, what evidence would help, and what could change as a result? That keeps the analysis connected to the people doing the work.

Build a usable foundation

Clear definitions, reliable records, access controls, and documentation make analysis repeatable. A process should still work when someone else has to run it.

Keep different measures distinct

Achievement is different from growth. Accounts are different from learners. Prototype results are different from live-process results. Those distinctions affect the decisions a number can support.

Make the result actionable

The output might be a revised lesson, a support process, a teacher conversation, or a better-organized knowledge base. The goal is to help someone do the next useful thing.

Tools I have used

Python, R, Excel, Google Sheets, Tableau, Google Workspace, and PowerSchool. The case studies identify the tools supported by the available record; they do not imply that every tool was used on every project.
