Paddlers Map

A map that brings rivers, water conditions, and canoe launches together for paddlers.

When I’m planning to paddle, I want to know where I can put in and what the water is doing. Gauge readings and river maps are useful, but they don’t always answer those questions together. Paddlers Map is my attempt to bring that information into one place.

The work includes gathering launch locations, working with river geometry, and connecting gauge readings to stretches of water. I’m using public sources including NOAA, USGS, and OpenStreetMap, and working through the differences in what those sources describe.

Making the map useful

The intended color scale runs from brown for lower water through blue for typical conditions, then yellow and red toward flood levels. A gauge reading still needs context: the map is a planning tool in development, and a water-level color by itself doesn’t establish whether a route is suitable to paddle.

Much of the work is in the data before it appears on the map: checking connections, separating launch access from unrelated places, and making enough river detail available without making the page slow.
